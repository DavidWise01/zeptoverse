Death-Roll Diodic Accretion

A grabs B; the composite captures D. Direction matters, ancestry survives, and divisions must partition rather than duplicate their roots. Five-part interactive research chapter.

1 · Formal operators

Diatomic — pair

A ↔ B → (A:B)

Diodic — directed capture

A ▷ B → AC
AC ▷ D → ACD

Divisive — partition

ACD → AC + D

AC is a state label; it does not mean that B vanished. The independent ancestry ledger records A, B, D.

2 · Conservation and three-witness accounting

lineage(AC)  = {A, B}
lineage(ACD) = {A, B, D}
count(roots before capture) = count(roots after capture)
No lineage root is duplicated by partition.

Three witnesses must describe independent observations, not three mirrors of one observation. This toy model treats named roots as provenance identifiers, not experimentally independent sensors.

3 · Branch/split rules

ACD → AC + D or ACD → A + CD are legal partitions when each original root appears in exactly one daughter branch. ACD → AC + CD is invalid if C is the same underlying root counted twice.

4 · Ice IX inspired lattice

An ideal hydrogen-bond network has four connections per water molecule, with two protons nearer and two farther. Directional transfers can be tested against this rule; the abstract accretion notation is not a proposed chemical mechanism for ice IX, nor evidence of Planck-scale quantum foam.
