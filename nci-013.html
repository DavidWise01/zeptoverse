Death-Roll Diodic Accretion

Directed capture with explicit ancestry, conservation, and structured release. The alligator analogy is a metaphor for asymmetric state transitions, not a literal description of molecular chemistry.

1 · Formal operators

Diatomic couples two sites: A ↔ B → (A:B). Diodic captures directionally: A ▷ B → AC; then AC ▷ D → ACD. Divisive splits a composite into conserved branches.

Important ancestry distinction: the labels AC and ACD are user-defined state names. The ledger separately records the original captured roots A, B, and D so renaming cannot erase B.

2 · Conservation and witnesses

root_budget(before) = root_budget(after)
lineage(AC) = {A, B}
lineage(ACD) = {A, B, D}
source ancestry = immutable root identities
[1/1] END END seals a completed checkpoint

3 · Branch and split

Split ACD into AC + D, or A + CD. The same root must never be counted twice. Splitting is a partition of a lineage set; physically changing the material would need additional energy and reaction rules.

4 · Ice IX lattice analogy

An ideal hydrogen-bonded water network has four bonds around each water: two protons near and two farther along the bonds (the ice rules). A directed capture is allowed only if it does not violate assigned bond occupation; changing proton-order configurations requires more than merely adding a new letter to a state name. Ice IX is a real ordered ice phase, but this accretion network is a mathematical toy model.
